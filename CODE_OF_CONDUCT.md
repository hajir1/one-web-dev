# Code of conduct

This document is adapted from the [Contributor Covenant 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/), with project-specific additions for OneWebDev.

## Our pledge

We, the members, contributors, and maintainers of OneWebDev, pledge to make participation in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, language, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community. OneWebDev is used by people who build websites for themselves, clients, and communities, often with very different levels of experience. Beginners are welcome here.

## Our standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:

- Being kind, patient, and respectful toward other people.
- Respecting differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback.
- Criticizing code, designs, and ideas rather than people.
- Taking responsibility for mistakes, apologizing to those affected, and learning from them.
- Helping newcomers: answering questions without condescension, and pointing to documentation when it exists.
- Focusing on what is best for the overall community, not only for oneself.
- Writing issues and pull requests that are clear, reproducible, and focused on one topic.
- Crediting other people's work, including templates, code, images, and ideas.
- Using the language you are comfortable with. Issues and pull requests in English or Bahasa Indonesia are both welcome; if you can, add a short English summary so more people can help.

Examples of unacceptable behavior:

- Sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment, including repeated unwanted contact or following someone.
- Publishing other people's private information, such as a physical address, email address, or tokens and credentials, without their explicit permission.
- Discrimination or hateful content aimed at a person or group.
- Spam, self-promotion unrelated to the discussion, or repeatedly pinging maintainers to demand a response.
- Submitting content you do not have the right to share, such as copyrighted templates, images, fonts, or code under an incompatible license.
- Submitting malicious code, hidden tracking, or deliberately misleading changes.
- Pressuring maintainers or contributors to accept a change, or to ignore this code of conduct.
- Other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Project-specific expectations

- **Issues:** search existing issues first, describe what you expected and what happened, and never paste real access tokens, passwords, or personal data. Remove secrets from logs and screenshots.
- **Pull requests:** keep them small and explain the reason for the change. Follow `CONTRIBUTING.md`. Review feedback is about the change, not about you.
- **Templates and assets:** only contribute work you created or have the right to license. Follow `LICENSE-TEMPLATES.md`, and do not copy designs from other products or brands.
- **Security:** report vulnerabilities privately, as described in `SECURITY.md`, and not in public issues or discussions.
- **Brand:** follow `TRADEMARK.md` when using the OneWebDev name or logo.
- **Disagreements:** it is fine to disagree on technical direction. Make your case with evidence, accept the maintainers' decision, and fork if you must. Forking is a normal part of open source.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies in all project spaces: the GitHub repository (issues, pull requests, discussions, and reviews), any chat or community channel run by the project, and project-related events. It also applies when an individual officially represents the project in public spaces, such as using an official project account or speaking as a project representative.

It can also apply to behavior outside these spaces if that behavior harms members of the community or is clearly linked to the project.

## Enforcement responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior, and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior they consider inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Maintainers have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned with this code of conduct, and will communicate the reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

Maintainers must respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any incident, and must not take part in decisions about incidents in which they are personally involved.

## Reporting

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be reported to the maintainers privately:

- **Email:** `REPLACE_WITH_CONDUCT_EMAIL`
- **GitHub:** the repository's **Security → Report a vulnerability** tab can also be used if no email is available. Mention that it is a code of conduct report.

Please include, as far as you can:

- who was involved, and links or screenshots of what happened;
- when and where it happened;
- any context that helps us understand the situation;
- whether you want to stay anonymous toward the person you are reporting.

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. We will acknowledge your report within 7 days. Reports are treated confidentially, and details are shared only with the people who need them to act.

## Enforcement guidelines

Maintainers will follow these guidelines in deciding the consequences of any action they deem in violation of this code of conduct.

### 1. Correction

**Impact:** use of inappropriate language or other behavior considered unprofessional or unwelcome.

**Consequence:** a private, written warning from the maintainers, with clarity about the violation and an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Impact:** a violation through a single incident or a series of actions.

**Consequence:** a warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing this code of conduct, for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding interaction in project spaces and on external channels. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary ban

**Impact:** a serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence:** a temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the project for a specified period of time. No public or private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing this code of conduct, is allowed during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Impact:** a pattern of violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence:** a permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the project community.

Content that violates the law, such as malware, stolen credentials, or infringing material, may be removed immediately and reported to the relevant platform or authority.

## Appeals

If you believe an enforcement decision was a mistake, you may appeal by writing to the same contact within 30 days of the decision. Include why you think the decision should change. A maintainer who was not part of the original decision will review it when possible. Decisions made on appeal are final.

## Good-faith reports

Reporting in good faith is always welcome, even if it turns out there was no violation. Retaliation against anyone who reports a problem or takes part in an investigation is itself a violation of this code of conduct.
